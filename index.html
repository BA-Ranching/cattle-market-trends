📋 About This Data — What It Shows & Where It Comes From

This dashboard pulls structured price data directly from the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS) MARS API. Understanding how that data is collected, structured, and aggregated will help you interpret the numbers accurately — especially when comparing this dashboard to the official USDA PDF reports.

🏛️ Data Source

All market data originates from the USDA AMS Market News Division and is accessed via the MARS API (marsapi.ams.usda.gov). The same underlying information appears in the human-readable PDF reports published at mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov.

Data is fetched automatically each weekday evening via a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow and cached as static JSON files. USDA typically publishes auction results the same day the sale concludes, so data is generally current within 24 hours of each auction.

⚙️ How the USDA MARS API Structures Its Data

The MARS API is designed specifically for price discovery — helping buyers and sellers understand what cattle of a given type and weight sold for. To do that reliably, it organizes data by standardized categories:

  • Commodity — Feeder Cattle, Slaughter Cattle, or Replacement Cattle
  • Class — Steers, Heifers, Bulls, Cows, Bred Cows, etc.
  • Weight bracket — e.g., 300–400 lbs, 400–500 lbs, and so on
  • Frame grade — Primarily Medium and Large 1 (M&L1), which is the predominant commercial grade

Each row in the API represents a specific commodity/class/weight-bracket combination with an average price ($/cwt or $/head) and head count for that grouping. This is exactly what drives every chart and table in this dashboard.

Key limitation: The API focuses on M&L1 frame cattle — the most common and widely-traded grade. Lower frame grades (M&L 1-2, Small & Large, etc.) are present in the auction PDF but are not always included as separate API rows. This means the API head counts will generally be lower than the PDF's "Total Receipts" figure.

📄 Why Dashboard Numbers May Differ from the USDA PDF Reports

There are three distinct reasons the numbers in this dashboard may not exactly match the official USDA PDF report for a given auction:

1. Frame grade filtering
The PDF reports cattle by every recognized USDA frame grade. For example, a typical San Angelo report will show separate tables for "Steers — Medium and Large 1" and "Steers — Medium and Large 1-2." The API structured data primarily returns M&L1 rows. The M&L 1-2 cattle — typically a smaller, somewhat lower-quality frame — run through the same ring but are not consistently represented in the API's machine-readable rows. The head count difference for those animals does not appear in this dashboard.

2. Unsold & passed cattle
Auctioneers track every animal run through the ring for the day's "Total Receipts" figure, including cattle that were passed (did not reach the minimum bid) or were withdrawn before sale. Since these animals have no transaction price, the API's price-reporting system has nothing to record. They are counted in the PDF's total receipts line but are invisible to this dashboard.

3. Commodity filter on the Market Comparison tab
The Market Comparison tab has a "Livestock Category" dropdown that filters to one commodity at a time (Feeder Cattle, Slaughter Cattle, or Replacement Cattle). Head counts shown on that tab reflect only the selected commodity — not all cattle at the auction. This is by design, to allow apples-to-apples market comparisons, but it means the number will always be smaller than total receipts.

Real-world example — San Angelo, April 2, 2026: The PDF reports "Total Receipts: 373" (307 Feeder + 62 Slaughter + 4 Replacement). The MARS API returns 305 total head across all commodities. The Market Comparison tab (filtered to Feeder Cattle) shows 266 head. All three figures are technically correct — they simply count different things.

💲 How Prices Are Calculated

All price figures in this dashboard are weighted average prices — meaning each lot's price is weighted by its head count before averaging. A lot of 30 steers carries more weight in the average than a lot of 3 steers. This is the same methodology USDA uses in the PDF reports.

Prices are expressed as:

  • $/cwt (dollars per hundredweight) for Feeder Cattle and Slaughter Cattle — the standard for cattle priced by the pound
  • $/head for Replacement Cattle (bred cows, replacement heifers) — these are typically sold as breeding animals at a flat per-head price rather than by weight

Weekly averages are computed by grouping all auction dates that fall within the same Sunday-through-Saturday week, then taking the weighted average across all markets and lots in that window. This means a week with a large auction will have greater influence on the weekly average than a week with a small run.

📅 Weekly Aggregation & Date Grouping

USDA auctions at different markets occur on different days throughout the week. To enable fair week-over-week trend comparisons, this dashboard groups all auction dates by the Sunday that starts that calendar week. For example, auction sales on Monday through Saturday of the same week are all grouped under the preceding Sunday's date.

This means that if two markets both held sales in the same week — one on Wednesday and one on Thursday — they will both appear under the same week label in trend charts, and their prices will be combined into a single weekly average. Weeks with only one market reporting will reflect that market's prices alone.

Note: If a market holds a special sale mid-week outside its normal schedule, that sale will be included in the same weekly bucket as its regular sale. This can occasionally produce a higher-than-normal head count for that week.

✅ What This Dashboard Is Reliable For

Despite the limitations above, the data here is highly reliable for its primary purpose — tracking price trends and market comparisons over time. Because the same subset of cattle (M&L1 frame, sold with reported prices) is captured consistently week after week and market after market, the trend lines are an accurate reflection of how prices are moving.

Use this dashboard for:

  • Monitoring week-over-week price direction for feeder, slaughter, and replacement cattle
  • Comparing price levels across different Texas auction markets
  • Identifying which weight brackets are trading at a premium or discount
  • Understanding seasonal price patterns and year-over-year comparisons
  • Tracking the steer-heifer price spread over time

For official total receipt counts, lot-level detail, or verification of specific transactions, always refer to the official USDA PDF report for that auction, available at mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov.
